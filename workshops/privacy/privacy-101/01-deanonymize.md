# Part 1: Deanonymize Me

## Introduction
An Ethereum address is a pseudonym, not an identity. Many people conclude that a fresh address is therefore private. In this part you play the analyst: the instructor has seeded Sepolia with transactions made by "users" trying to hide, and you must link them back together.

Everything you break here is something a later part of the workshop fixes. Keep notes on *why* each trick worked.

* **Network:** Sepolia
* **Grading:** Evaluator, **8 points**
* **Tools:** a block explorer, `cast` (Foundry) or a short ethers.js script

## What Leaks on a Transparent Chain
For every transaction, anyone can read the sender, the recipient, the amount, the calldata, the gas price and the block timestamp, forever. Analysts combine these with a few heuristics:

* **Funding link.** A new address needs ETH for gas. Whoever sent it that first ETH is usually its owner, or knows them.
* **Amount fingerprint.** An unusual amount (`0.013370 ETH`) going into a contract and the same amount coming out identifies the pair.
* **Timing correlation.** A withdrawal that follows a deposit by a few blocks, when nobody else deposited in between, is probably the same user.
* **Common ownership.** Addresses that later send their funds to the same destination, or that are spent together, are probably controlled by the same person.
* **Behavioural fingerprint.** Gas price settings, wallet-specific calldata, activity hours.

## How to Work on This Part
The workshop ships with these contracts (addresses in the [README](./README.md#deployed-addresses)):
- `NaiveMixer`: accepts deposits of **any** amount and lets anyone holding the matching secret withdraw to any address.
- `FixedMixer`: same idea, but every deposit is exactly `0.01 ETH`.
- `Evaluator`: holds one challenge per exercise and checks your answer.

For each exercise, read your challenge with `evaluator.getChallenge(exerciseId, yourAddress)`. It returns the address or transaction hash you start from. Submit the address you found with the matching `exN_...` function.

Rules:
- You have **3 attempts** per exercise. Investigate first, submit second.
- Scenarios are assigned per student address. Your neighbour's answer is not yours.
- No special tooling is required. Every answer can be found with a block explorer.

## Points List

### Exercise 1: follow the gas (1 point)
Your challenge is a "burner" address that made a single anonymous donation. Find the address that owns it.

1. Look at the first incoming transaction of the burner.
2. Call `ex1_fundingLink(owner)`.

### Exercise 2: amount fingerprint (2 points)
Your challenge is a withdrawal transaction from `NaiveMixer`. Find the address that made the matching deposit.

1. List the `Deposit` events of `NaiveMixer`.
2. Compare amounts with your withdrawal.
3. Call `ex2_amountFingerprint(depositor)`.

### Exercise 3: timing correlation (2 points)
Your challenge is a withdrawal transaction from `FixedMixer`. All amounts are identical now, so amounts no longer help. Find the depositor.

1. List deposits and withdrawals in block order.
2. Work out which deposits were still unspent just before your withdrawal.
3. Call `ex3_timing(depositor)`.

### Exercise 4: the recipient pays for its own gas (1 point)
Your challenge is a `FixedMixer` withdrawal sent to a brand new address, at a time when many deposits were pending. Timing does not help. Find the depositor anyway.

1. Withdrawing requires a transaction, and a transaction requires gas. Who paid for it, and where did that ETH come from?
2. Call `ex4_gasFunder(depositor)`.

### Exercise 5: clustering (2 points)
Your challenge is a list of five `FixedMixer` withdrawal recipients. Some of them belong to the same person. Report how many distinct people are behind them.

1. Follow what each recipient does with its funds afterwards.
2. Call `ex5_cluster(numberOfPeople)`.

## Questions
Answer these in your repository. They are not graded on-chain, but each one is the motivation for a later part.

1. In exercise 2, what single change to the mixer would have defeated you? (See part 8.)
2. In exercise 3, how many other deposits would the user have needed to wait for to be one-in-ten? What is this number called?

## Notes and Hints
- Listing events with Foundry:
  ```bash
  cast logs --rpc-url $SEPOLIA_RPC_URL --address <mixer> --from-block <deployBlock> "Deposit(address,uint256)"
  ```
- Internal transfers (ETH sent by a contract) do not appear in the normal transaction list of an address. Use the "Internal Transactions" tab of the explorer.
- The scenarios were generated by a script. Real users are messier, and real analysts combine several heuristics at once.
